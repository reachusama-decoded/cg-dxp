# Lesson plans for CG Core

A lesson plan is what an instructor delivers a session from: what to do, what to say and how to pace a five-hour day (user, 2026-11-02), with the few facts they might need close at hand. CG Core's lesson plans adapt Decoded's lesson plan template (`reference/Lesson Plan Template [MAKE A COPY].md`) to this programme (user, 2026-10-16). Each session has one, `courseware/<unit>/dayNN-<slug>/instructor/module00-overview/dayNN_lesson_plan.md`. The Markdown is the source, reviewed and audited like the rest of the session. The build turns it into `release/latest/<unit>/dayNN-<slug>/instructor/module00-overview/dayNN_lesson_plan.docx`, the Word document instructors get (A4 landscape, by `scripts/docx/build_lesson_plan.js`); there is no lesson-plan PDF. Nobody edits the Word file; changes go into the Markdown and the document is rebuilt.

- **The exact format**, with an example of every part: [references/format.md](references/format.md). Read it before you write or check a plan.
- **A blank plan** to copy when you start a new session: [assets/lesson-plan-template.md](assets/lesson-plan-template.md).
- **The welcome day** (user, 2026-11-02): `unit00-programme/day00-welcome/instructor/module00-overview/day00_lesson_plan.md` is a 15-minute plan in the same format with one Timeline section, `### Welcome {#day00-welcome}`, five rows on the 2.5-minute grid from 0:00 to 0:15 (user, 2026-11-02): Welcome and why we're here (Intro/Outro, 2.5) · Five units: choose the ones you need (Explain, 2.5) · How a day runs and getting help (Explain, 2.5) · Is this the right programme for you (Questioning, 2.5) · Introductions (Independent or pairs work, 5); there is no Over to the day row (user, 2026-11-02), the Overview, Objectives and outcomes, Before the session, Question bank, FAQs and Activity types sections, and no Curriculum map, module sections, intro/outro rows or anchors. Instructors run it inside each unit's first day's 15-minute `Welcome` row, 0:05 to 0:20 (user, 2026-11-02): the day's plan has the row, and `scripts/make_slides.py` drafts one Welcome slide for it in that day's deck, `dayNN-welcome`, while the welcome keeps its own deck, `unit00-programme/day00-welcome/instructor/module00-overview/day00_slides.pptx`, which instructors run beside the day's deck, moving slides across themselves if they want (user, 2026-11-02: kept separate). The audit checks it in welcome-day mode.

## Teach from the back of the room

The rule behind everything here (user, 2026-10-16): instructors explain less, and learners spend the time in the challenges.

- **Lean rows.** A row is read in a glance: a line of purpose, a line of what's on screen, up to three short notes, up to two lines to say, a line of transition, and an assessment of two short parts. Anything longer belongs in the notebooks, which already carry the demo's beats and the challenge's tasks; each demo beat still gets a note and a Say of its own in the demo rows, each opening with its heading in bold.
- **Challenges go as far as you can.** No tier is mandatory. Learners start at Q1 and stop where the time stops; nobody is chased, and there are no check-ins every ten minutes: one 2.5-minute Questioning row mid-challenge is the only pause. The instructor stays available.
- **Debriefs are short and useful.** Walk through the solution in `challenge-solution/`, or ask two or three questions that surface blockers.
- **Put it together is always optional** (user, 2026-10-16): the stretch for fast finishers, inside a last block of open challenge time, never a block everyone does.

## Where it sits

The flow handbook → lesson plan → overview → demo → challenges says what each file is written from (`module-builder`). The lesson plan is written once the user has approved every handbook of the session, together with the overview, demo and challenges for all the modules (user, 2026-10-26), and checked against `module-builder/references/session-checklist.md` (items 17 and 19). So:

- Write a module's rows from its **handbook**: the concepts, their order, the names and numbers. The handbook is the source of truth.
- The **overview** is written from the lesson plan, but it owns the outcomes, the self-check and the session questions. Once it exists, copy the outcomes into "Objectives and outcomes" word for word.
- When the **demo** and **challenge** exist, go back and fill in what depends on them: each row's "On screen", what a finished challenge looks like, the likeliest stalls, the facts to have ready and the pre-runs (each demo step too slow for its row). Run them and copy their real output: an instructor checks the room against those numbers.
- Read the previous session's lesson plan for the bridge in, and `internal-programme-overview.md` for the curriculum map and the session's place in it.

## The shape

The sections, in order (details and examples in the format reference):

1. Title block: content title, breadcrumb ("… › Day NN — lesson plan for instructors"), then "Timings are a guide: adapt them to the room." and the line on confirming the timetable.
2. **Overview**: where the session sits, the Jordan thread, the themes (one per module, then "**Open challenge time (optional).**", naming Put it together as the stretch), what learners leave with, who it's for.
3. **Objectives and outcomes**: one row per outcome (objective, outcome word for word, module, evidence; a clause evidenced only in the demo or debrief says so), then which module answers which session question. That mapping lives only here.
4. **Curriculum map**: every session with its modules, this one marked.
5. **Before the session**: materials, a short checklist in three groups, Before the day, On the day and After rehearsing (copy the course folder to your laptop and work in that copy, the dry run, what to start early and the images to pull, the pre-runs in each break, the slow-network plan, the room reset), one line an item with any command or file list as a sub-bullet (instructors still prepare before the day), then the learner setup check (learners set up at the start of the day, as they arrive; you check it at the end of the welcome; user, 2026-10-19). No "Needs CG access" part: the session runs on local containers (`programme-profile` rule 3).
6. **Timeline**: the template's lesson plan table, written as section rows (H3) and activity rows (H4).
7. **Module N reference** for each module, and **Put it together reference**: short, a line per tier of a finished challenge, the three likeliest stalls, two or three fallbacks, the facts to have at hand.
8. **Question bank** (per module: its key questions, then two or three check questions), **Support and stretch**, **FAQs**, **Activity types**.

Leave out the apprenticeship parts of Decoded's template that the format reference lists (Key links, KSBs, functional skills and the rest). CG Core is a corporate programme, so they don't apply, and the user asked for them to go; the audit flags any that come back. The template's housekeeping, feedback form and next-session preview stay, briefly, in the welcome and the wrap-up.

## The timeline

Instructors read the timeline mid-session, glancing between the room and the page. Every rule here serves that glance.

- **The day.** A session is five hours, 0:00 to 5:00 (300 minutes; user, 2026-11-02). The start and end take 45 minutes on the template (the 25-minute intro and the 20-minute wrap-up, the closing debrief in it; user, 2026-11-02), breaks aside; every module, with all its activities, is 75 minutes, at 0:25, 1:40 and 2:55 (user, 2026-11-02); open challenge time is 30 minutes at 4:10.
- **Each module's 75 minutes** (user, 2026-11-02), in this order:
  - demo, about 20: one Instructor-led demo row per demo `### ` heading, its title that heading word for word, in the demo's order, in 2.5-minute steps (user, 2026-10-27; nothing skipped or grouped), so an instructor always knows which section of the notebook they're on. The module map (heading, 🧭 anchor, bridge, map) sits in the first row, whose first Say asks the anchor's question; the module's closing lines are the end of the last beat, not a heading, and that row's On screen reads `<heading>, to its closing lines`; every row has a note and a Say opening with its heading in bold (`references/format.md` → Demo rows);
  - Questioning, 2.5: straight after the demo's last beat, one row of a question or two to the room on what the demo showed; its minutes come from the demo, by shortening one 5-minute beat to 2.5 (user, 2026-10-27);
  - challenge, 20 to 25: two Independent or pairs work rows, a brief of a line or two and then learners work, then learners still working with the instructor available; in paired work, partners work in parallel, each on their own laptop and copy, and swap only for the review, and the brief says so. Between the two rows sits the module's second Questioning row, 2.5 minutes taken from the second row: a mid-challenge check-in of a question or two on the learners' own work, hands up for a moment, then back to it;
  - shareback/questions, 15 (user, 2026-11-02, replacing the same day's 10-minute Catch-up and questions row): one Feedback and discussion row titled `Shareback/questions`, exactly 15 minutes, after the challenge's rows and straight before the debrief: learners share what they found and where they stuck, and ask anything; the instructor takes the questions. A module with a `Catch-up and questions` row fails the audit;
  - debrief, 5: one Feedback and discussion row;
  - break, 5 to 10: one Break row whose Transition is the bridge into the next block, and whose notes list the pre-runs for the next module's demo (a push, a pipeline run, a first image build), so every demo row fits its minutes on the day.
  A module may run a little faster or slower with its complexity, keeping that pattern; the demo's 🏆 CHALLENGE TIME box gives the challenge's minutes.
- **The last block** is optional open challenge time, 30 minutes at 4:10, Independent or pairs work rows only: learners carry on with any module challenge, as far as they can, or take Put it together, the stretch for fast finishers. Then the wrap-up, 20 minutes (user, 2026-11-02): Closing debrief and questions (10: blockers, anything shipped, the questions the day has left), Key takeaways (5), then Thank you for participating (5), to 5:00.
- **Rows.** `#### H:MM · N min · <Activity type> — <Title>`, where `H:MM` is the start time counted from 0:00 (`H:MM:SS` on a half minute) and `N` is a step of 2.5 from 2.5 to 20. One demo row per demo `### ` heading, its title that heading word for word, joined by ` · `. Each row starts where the previous one ended, and the last row ends at the session's length.
- **Times in 2.5-minute steps** (user, 2026-10-27, replacing 2026-10-16's fives): demo rows run 2.5, 5, 7.5 … so every heading gets its own row and the demo still totals about 20 minutes; the Questioning rows and the intro's Jordan and the day and What you will be able to do run 2.5 (user, 2026-11-02), and other blocks keep five-minute lengths. Start times on a half minute are written `0:12:30`. They're a guide and instructors adapt them.
- **Activity types**, exactly these seven: Intro/Outro, Explain, Instructor-led demo, Questioning, Independent or pairs work, Feedback and discussion, Break. A type says *how* the time is spent; the section and the title say what it's about.
- **Fields**, as bold labels in this order: Purpose, On screen, From the overview, Instructor notes, Ask, Listen for, If stuck, Say, Transition, Assessment, Adapt. Ask, Listen for and If stuck belong to Questioning rows only, which have them in place of notes and Say.
  - Purpose: one line (not on a break).
  - On screen: one line, where something is showing: the file first, by its path from the session folder, then the section and beats (`` `moduleNN-<slug>/dayNN_mN_demo.ipynb`: <heading> ``, `` `moduleNN-<slug>/dayNN_mN_challenge.ipynb`: … ``; user, 2026-10-19; module folders 2026-10-27).
  - From the overview (user, 2026-10-27): the two intro rows only. *Jordan and the day* holds the overview's persona card grid table and *What you will be able to do* its lead line "By the end of this session you will be able to:" and the outcome bullets, word for word. Sync runs overview → lesson plan → slides; the overview stays the source.
  - Instructor notes: up to three short bullets (every row).
  - Ask (Questioning rows): one or two questions, as bullets, each a line, put to the room as written.
  - Listen for (Questioning rows): one line, what a good answer contains.
  - If stuck (Questioning rows): one line, the prompt or simpler question that gets the room there.
  - Say: up to two brief lines, in Decoded's tone of voice (optional; Key takeaways has three, one per module).
  - Transition: one line (every row but the last).
  - Assessment: two short parts, `Outcome N · <evidence>` ("Outcomes 2, 3 · remote exit codes, the `REPORT_DATA` change"), or `—` (every row). The column is narrow (user, 2026-10-19), so keep it to about 15 words.
  - Adapt: one line, only where it really matters.
  Leave out an optional field rather than writing "none". Key questions never go in a row: they belong to the module's part of the Question bank. A Questioning row's Ask is the exception, because it's what the instructor asks there and then.
- **Section rows**: one H3 each for the welcome, each module, open challenge time and the wrap-up, each with a one- or two-sentence section summary (the outcomes served, the session questions answered, what learners make). A module's heading is `### Module N — <canonical title> {#dayNN-mN}`, the programme overview's title word for word, and straight under it comes **Module outcomes.**: a bullet per outcome the Objectives table maps to the module, `Outcome N · ` then the overview's outcome word for word (user, 2026-10-27). Then its 🧭 anchor (the `::: {.anchor}` div that `scripts/nbkit.py anchors` generates from `dayNN_anchors.md`, never typed; the Word build draws it), and the first Say of the module's first demo row asks the anchor's question.
- **Standard items**: the welcome is a mandatory 25-minute intro, every day (user, 2026-11-02), four rows: **Arrive and settle** (Intro/Outro, 5; learners open the course folder in its dev container and start the services while they settle, the instructor walks the room, housekeeping; the deck's standard Arrive and settle slide), **Welcome** (Intro/Outro, 15) on a unit's first day, Day 01, 03, 05 and 07, where the welcome day's own plan runs inside the row from its own deck, beside the day's (one drafted slide, `dayNN-welcome`; user, 2026-11-02: kept separate), or **Welcome back** (Intro/Outro, 15) on its second day, Day 02, 04, 06 and 08 (yesterday's list and today's thread; one drafted slide), **Jordan and the day** (Explain, 2.5; From the overview: the persona card) and **What you will be able to do** (Explain, 2.5; From the overview: the outcomes; the notes carry the self-check pacing). No Setup check row (setup is pre-work in the overview, user 2026-10-27) and no Meet and greet row (introductions live in the welcome). Outcomes appear once, here: no **Module outcomes.** under a module's heading (user, 2026-10-27). The wrap-up is always three rows, ending the day (user, 2026-11-02): **Closing debrief and questions** (10 minutes, Feedback and discussion: blockers, anything shipped, the questions the day has left); **Key takeaways** (5 minutes, Feedback and discussion), its first note a very brief look back ("Look back first, briefly: read the outcome verbs, a hand for each."), three Say lines, one per module, the takeaway in a sentence; then **Thank you for participating** (5 minutes, Intro/Outro), the thanks, the feedback form and the next session's preview, Assessment `—`.

S01, the example (the five-hour day, user 2026-11-02): welcome 0:00–0:25 (Arrive and settle 5, Welcome 15, Jordan and the day 2.5, What you will be able to do 2.5; Day 02 has Welcome back); Module 1 0:25–1:40, Module 2 1:40–2:55, Module 3 2:55–4:10, each demo 2.5-minute beats to about 17.5, Questioning 2.5, challenge 15, Questioning 2.5, learners keep working 7.5, Shareback/questions 15, debrief 5, break 10 (user, 2026-11-02); open challenge time 4:10–4:40 in rows of 20 and 10, Put it together the optional stretch; Closing debrief and questions 4:40–4:50; Key takeaways 4:50–4:55; Thank you for participating 4:55–5:00.

The timeline always covers the whole session, 0:00 to the end, and every module's rows are written together, from the approved handbooks, so no row waits for a later module.

## Writing it well

- **Decoded's voice.** Plain, warm and direct, in British English: `courseware-conventions/references/tone-of-voice-guidelines.txt` and `writing-rules.md`. No wasted words, no hedging, no jargon for its own sake. Instructor notes are short imperative bullets. Say lines are what a person says in one breath, not a script. A purpose is one line about what learners get from the row.
- **Point, don't copy.** A row names what's on screen by notebook section and visual ("the demo notebook's Module 1 section") or by box title ("the COPILOT box 'Turn the notebook into a script'"), not by cell number. Learner material lives in the anchors; the plan says what to do with it, and never retells a demo beat by beat: a demo row names each beat by its `### ` heading, in its title and in bold at the start of the beat's notes and Say, and says what to run, what to expect and what to say, while the notebook carries the bullets.
- **Show a finished version in the debrief** (user, 2026-10-16). A debrief row may put the completed challenge on screen: the module's `challenge-solution/` notebook, or its PDF in that module's instructor folder, named in the row's On screen by the notebook, `` `challenge-solution/dayNN_mN_challenge.ipynb`, Module N: <task boxes> ``, since instructors show it in VS Code (user, 2026-10-19), and pointed at by section and task box; the checklist says to open it, or its PDF, for the debriefs. It's shown, never handed out. `challenge-solution/` sits in each instructor module folder, one level deeper than the learner module folder, with the same file names as its challenge files, so its paths into `common/` go up one more level (five) (user, 2026-10-19; 2026-10-27); it runs as it is and the checklist has no step to copy it; the Materials table has its row. Its numbers are the same as the plan's "What a finished challenge looks like", because both come from the folded solutions.
- **Specific and defined** (user, 2026-10-27). A note or Say line names the file and the command ("run `python -m pytest -q`: 9 passed"), never "the check". The plan defines a term at its first use before any shorthand for it (test-driven development (TDD) before "test-first"), as the anchors do, and a Say line never reads as hand-coding (learners prompt Copilot and review).
- **Facts must be real.** Every number, file name, error line and output must match what the demo and challenge print today in the dev container image. Run them; don't recall them.
- **Programme rules that apply here:**
  - The plan may name "the Module N handbook" as a whole, never one of its sections.
  - "Challenge" names the learner activity.
  - Jordan Mensah is they/them. No colleague's name appears.
  - No absolute paths.
  - A prompt quoted in full sits in a `::: copilot` div.
  - No CG-access notes: no "Needs CG access" section, `::: blocked` callout, "until CG answers" caveat, mirror-container fallback or Asana task. The session runs on the dev container and the local services, and the plan says "the dev container", never "the CG dev container" (`programme-profile` rule 3; user, 2026-10-16).
  - The person delivering is "the instructor" (user, 2026-10-16; the word since 2026-10-27).
  - Rows point to the notebooks and the overview, never to slide numbers: the deck is drawn from the plan (see Build and check), and only the Arrive and settle, Welcome and Welcome back rows name their slides.
  - FAQs and comparisons give pros and cons and what each is good for, never a tool's limitations (CG rule 2).
- **Placeholders.** The blank plan's placeholders are written ‹like this›, never `<like this>`, which pandoc reads as HTML and the Word build rejects. A finished plan has none; the audit flags any left.
- **Question bank.** Per module, its key questions from the brief, then two or three questions, each checking one idea the module teaches, with the correct answer in bold, each tied to the row it follows ("Ask after").

## Converting an older plan

Plans written before the lean format (the first 2026-10-16 format, whose rows ran five or ten minutes, or older plans with headings per block) move into it like this. Keep every fact an instructor relies on; drop the retelling the notebooks already carry.

| Before | Now |
|---|---|
| A 10- or 20-minute welcome row, the morning's 40-minute intro, a Setup check row, a Meet and greet row, or **Module outcomes.** under a module heading | The 25-minute intro (user, 2026-11-02): Arrive and settle (5), Welcome on a unit's first day or Welcome back on its second (15), Jordan and the day (2.5), What you will be able to do (2.5); outcomes once, in that intro |
| A module of an hour, from the challenge straight into the debrief, or of 70 minutes with a 10-minute Catch-up and questions row | 75 minutes, with a 15-minute Shareback/questions row (Feedback and discussion) after the challenge's rows and straight before the debrief (user, 2026-11-02) |
| A module's Key questions, in its section row or its rows | The module's part of the Question bank |
| One 20-minute Instructor-led demo row, or Explain and demo rows grouping the handbook's beats | One Instructor-led demo row per demo `### ` heading, titled with it, in 2.5-minute steps, a note and a Say per row, each labelled with its heading in bold |
| Points for each visual, the demo walk-through, in the reference | Gone from the reference: the demo rows carry each beat |
| Three challenge rows (10, 10, 5) with timed walk-rounds and a five-minute warning | Two rows: a brief, then learners working, with one 2.5-minute Questioning row between them |
| A debrief of counts, takeaways and several Say lines | One 5-minute row: walk through the solution, or two or three questions that surface blockers |
| A put-it-together hour everyone does, or a closing debrief inside open challenge time | Open challenge time, 30 minutes at 4:10, with Put it together the optional stretch; the closing debrief opens the Wrap-up (user, 2026-11-02) |
| One 10-minute wrap-up row | Closing debrief and questions (10, Feedback and discussion), Key takeaways (5, Feedback and discussion), then Thank you for participating (5, Intro/Outro), to 5:00 |
| No questions to the room in a module | Two 2.5-minute Questioning rows: after the demo, and between the challenge's two rows |
| Long stalls and fallbacks tables | The three likeliest stalls, two or three fallbacks, each a line |

Retime in 2.5-minute steps as you go, and move old minute marks to the nearest row. When a fact appears twice, keep it once, in the place an instructor would look for it.
