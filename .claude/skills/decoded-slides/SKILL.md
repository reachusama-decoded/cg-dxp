# Decoded slides

The master is [references/slide-templates-library.txt](references/slide-templates-library.txt) — a text export of the 2026 guidelines and template library. Read it before laying out any slide or brochure page; never edit it.

## How to use it

1. Decide the format first: **presentation** (to be presented from: talks, high-level proposals — minimal text, visual information) or **brochure** (to be read: reports, long-hand proposals — more space for text).
2. Read the universal principles section, then pick from the key templates; go to further templates only when none of the key ones fit.
3. Apply the voice rules from `courseware-conventions/references/tone-of-voice-guidelines.txt`: clarity over complexity, no hedging, no jargon, one key takeaway per slide.
4. Brand colours and the diagram palette are in `courseware-conventions/references/brand-components.md`.
5. If the deck accompanies a session, the lesson plan's `> **Slide N** |` references must match the slide numbers you ship; build the deck from the lesson plan's What-to-say beats, not the other way round.

When the deliverable is a `.pptx` file, pair this skill with the pptx skill for the mechanics; this skill decides what goes on each slide and how it looks.

**CG Core:** slides come last, one deck a day, drafted from the reviewed lesson plan by `scripts/make_slides.py` and kept in step with `sync` (user, 2026-10-27). Its order (user, 2026-11-02): cover · Arrive and settle (the standard slide, the same in every unit and day, id `arrive-and-settle`, for the plan's Arrive and settle row: `full-visual`, the usual title at the top over the shared picture `common/figures/shared/setup-check.png` on yellow (alt text as an attribute, `![](…){alt="…"}`, so no caption is drawn) and no other text, its notes the setup cue; drawn by `scripts/figures/shared_setup_check.py` with `brand.slide_figure()`) · the welcome: on a unit's first day (a `Welcome` row) one drafted `dayNN-welcome` title-body slide, Welcome, while the welcome itself runs from its own deck, `day00_slides.pptx`, beside the day's (user, 2026-11-02: kept separate); on its second day (a `Welcome back` row) one drafted `dayNN-welcome-back` title-body slide · Jordan and the day (Decoded's `table` layout: a short phrase a row, the card word for word in the notes) · What you will be able to do (a table: a short phrase per outcome, each outcome word for word in the notes; outcomes appear once a day) · Today (`agenda-3` tiles on the yellow photograph: "Module 01", the full canonical title, never shortened, then the module in one short line, the anchors file's **In short:**; a title that needs three lines is built with a stand-in and placed by `make_slides.py` with its caption moved down, user 2026-10-27) · per module: divider (`section-divider-photo`, the same yellow photograph as Today: "#1" and the canonical title) · the module's question (`statement`) · What, why and how (`recap-3` tiles, the anchors file's short phrases; the anchor word for word in the notes) · map · one slide per demo heading · a think slide (the first Ask's question as the title, its lead-in and any second Ask as prompts) · challenge brief and continuation · Shareback/questions (`challenge-continuation`, id `dayNN-mN-shareback`, drafted from the row's On screen points or Purpose, hand-tunable; user, 2026-11-02; sync retires the old `dayNN-mN-catch-up`) · debrief · break · Put it together · Closing debrief and questions (the Wrap-up's first row, id `dayNN-closing-debrief`) · Key takeaways · Thank you for participating (title and photo only; the feedback form stays in the notes, never on the slide); no Meet and greet or Setup check slide, no day-level question or what, why and how slide. **Concise slides** (user, 2026-10-27, like Fundamentals'): a title of 8 words or fewer (canonical titles and a statement slide's question exempt), at most 3 bullets a slot, each bullet, line or table cell 12 words or fewer; the rest goes in the speaker notes (**Detail:**, **Say:**), so nothing is lost. The generator drafts to it (`short`, `concise`) and `make_slides.py check` and the audit fail on longer text (`concise_problems`). Titles are the programme overview's, word for word: a long one gets a smaller font, never a cut. The full rules are in `lesson-build` → File shapes → Slides; use this skill's layout and voice rules when tuning the deck. The slides never determine the curriculum. CG Core lesson plans carry no slide numbers (no `> **Slide N** |` lines), so step 5 doesn't apply there: an instructor delivers from the lesson plan and the notebooks.

**CG Core outcomes slide and wording (user, 2026-11-02).** The day's "What you will be able to do" slide carries each outcome's short form, the words before its " — " in `internal-programme-overview.md` §6, word for word, never a paraphrase; its table cells may run to 25 words (`OUTCOME_WORDS`), the full outcome in the notes. `make_slides.py` drafts the short forms and the audit fails a drift. Every slide line is clear on its own: no pronoun without a named antecedent on the slide ("a pipeline that feeds them" names what it feeds). Unit cards (`scripts/figures/day00/unit_cards.py`) take their day titles from the design document, grow each box to the longest text and wrap at whole words.

**Concrete promises (user, 2026-11-02).** Never promise that learners can "trust" or "defend" something; say what they do that earns it (tested, scheduled, safe to rerun; checked against the source data; reviewed and checked on every push; presented, with questions answered). The exact technical senses stay (SSH host keys, checking Copilot's output).

**Action-first prose (user, 2026-11-02).** Summaries, purposes, section summaries, slide lines, cards and speaker notes say what learners do, in order, with a concrete object ("Write pytest tests for the report's code, fix it test-first…"), never a topic summary ("Make a change the way a team does", "An AI service that answers with evidence"). The rule and its banned words are in `courseware-conventions/references/writing-rules.md` → Learner-facing descriptions; reviews flag any line a reader couldn't picture doing at their laptop.

**Technical terms and completeness (user, 2026-11-02).** A summary names the techniques in the field's terms (FastAPI, RAG, agents, evals, dbt, Airflow), explained at first use or in the notes, and covers every module it summarises; check it against the module list ("Build a FastAPI service with model calls, RAG, agents and evals", not "Serve a JSON API whose AI summaries cite sources"). Rule: `courseware-conventions/references/writing-rules.md` → Learner-facing descriptions.
