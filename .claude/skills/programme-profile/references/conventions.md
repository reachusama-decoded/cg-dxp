# CG Core content conventions

Programme-specific rulings. Decoded-wide rules live in `courseware-conventions`; this file holds only what is true for CG Core. Dated entries are user- or MJB-confirmed; undated entries are the brief's.

## Voice and register

- UK English, sentence case, plain sentences, no hedging, no corporate jargon. The tone-of-voice master applies, except that handbook and overview prose is written in connected paragraphs, not Decoded's short-paragraph style (see "Writing rules" below).
- The reader is an engineer. Say what a tool lets them do, then show it. Plain-English introduction at first use for every term the pair has not taught.
- Em dashes are allowed in prose (the brief uses them); keep them sparing and never as a substitute for a full stop. Do not run a dash-removal pass on Core.
- Pronouns: they/them for Jordan Mensah and every invented person (user-confirmed 2026-10-13).
- Second person for learner-facing text ("you"), first person plural only for the room ("we'll run this together" is fine in the lesson plan, not in learner text).

## What every module carries (user, 2026-10-15)

Developed in this order, each written from the one before: **handbook → lesson plan → overview → demo → challenges** ([layout.md](layout.md)). Built in this order (user, 2026-10-26): every handbook of the session first, through the source process, approved by the user; then the lesson plan, overview, demo and challenges for all the modules together. Every session is built and checked against `module-builder/references/session-checklist.md`.

- **A handbook** `learner/moduleNN-<slug>/dayNN_mN_handbook.md`: a self-contained concept text in the style of the L6 handbook (`reference/L6-source content/M09 … Handbook (WIP).md`). One H1 with the id `{#dayNN-mN-handbook}`, plain H2 and H3 headings with no typed numbers (the PDF numbers them), every figure captioned by its image description (or a `<!-- caption: "…" -->` comment after a Mermaid fence), worked examples on the demo data with Jordan and real tested output, an "Optional further reading" subsection closing each H2 section (items `- **Type:** [*Title*](url) by Source — what it adds.`), and a `## Summary {… .unnumbered}` last, followed only by an optional appendix (`## Going further … {… .unnumbered}`, which opens by saying nothing in it is needed for the module, its exercises or its outcomes). It opens with an intro paragraph, the persona card and `## What this handbook covers {#dayNN-mN-covers .unnumbered}`, and is the concepts in depth, never saying when to read it. It is the source of truth for concepts, names, numbers and order. It never mentions demos, challenges, the session, the agenda, setup, instructors or Asana, and never promises a later module; it may build on concepts from earlier handbooks.
- **Its part of the lesson plan** in `instructor/module00-overview/dayNN_lesson_plan.md`, in the `lesson-plan` skill's format (user, 2026-10-16): a section row headed by the module's canonical title, then its 🧭 anchor (no **Module outcomes.**: outcomes appear once, in the intro; user, 2026-10-27), and the module's hour in lean rows (user, 2026-10-16, with the correction the same afternoon): demo about 20, a note and a Say for each beat, challenge 20 to 25 in two rows (a brief, then learners working), debrief 5, break 5 to 10; every `### ` heading of the demo's module section is exactly one lesson-plan row, with the same title and in the same order, nothing skipped or grouped; the closing ("Where the report goes next") is the last lines of the final beat, before CHALLENGE TIME, not a heading; the module's section title in the plan equals the demo's `## Module N — …` heading; the challenge rows name the challenge's `## … challenge — …` title and its Q1, Q2 and Q3 task titles exactly; rows of 2.5 to 20 minutes in 2.5-minute steps, start times h:mm (or h:mm:ss on a half minute); the audit fails on drift (user, 2026-10-27); each row a line of purpose and on screen, up to three short notes, up to two brief lines to say, a line of transition and of assessment, Adapt only where it matters; then its short reference section (a line per tier of a finished challenge, the three likeliest stalls, two or three fallbacks, the facts to have at hand) and its part of the Question bank. Instructor text may name modules and say the timetable is working.
- **Its slides**, after the lesson plan (user, 2026-10-27): the module's divider, its question (a statement slide), what/why/how (three `recap-3` tiles, the anchors file's short phrases), map, one slide per demo heading, challenge brief, debrief and break (no module objectives slide; user, 2026-10-27), all of it concise: titles of 8 words or fewer, at most 3 bullets of 12 words or fewer, the rest in the notes in the day's `instructor/module00-overview/dayNN_slides.md`, drafted by `scripts/make_slides.py` and tuned by hand; learner-facing on screen (no later session named as where something is taught), instructor detail in the speaker notes.
- **Its place in `learner/module00-overview/dayNN_overview.md`**: the outcomes it serves (outcomes live only there, from `internal-programme-overview.md` §6), its agenda row, its questions and at most one self-check question (about the prior knowledge that most changes the module's pace), none of them carrying a module tag (user, 2026-10-15); the lesson plan holds the mapping and its welcome says what the module's self-check question paces.
- **A demo section** in the module demo notebook (`instructor/moduleNN-<slug>/dayNN_mN_demo.ipynb`) on the demo data, opening with its 🧭 anchor and a bridge naming what it builds on (below), and ending with a 🏆 CHALLENGE TIME box then its Go deeper box, plus any module-prefixed files beside the anchor.
- **A challenge section** in the module challenge notebook (`learner/moduleNN-<slug>/dayNN_mN_challenge.ipynb`) on the challenge data: one task in AIA's box set that learners take as far as they can in the module's challenge time, about 20 to 25 minutes (a ⚡ Q1 · CORE, Q2 · STRETCH and Q3 · BONUS task box; everyone starts at Q1, no tier is mandatory, Q3 is not expected), a 💭 REFLECT box before each folded solution, 🎯 YOUR TURN boxes for short no-code tasks, then its Go deeper box. It stands alone (its own setup cell, data path and starter files; never a demo cell, file or variable) and follows "Learners direct AI and review what it wrote" below. Its planted faults are its own, never the demo's; each outcome clause's core is evidenced at Q1 or Q2 (a clause evidenced only in the demo or debrief is said so in the lesson plan); a module that works on a project starts from its own folder (`mN_<project>/`) in `demo/` and `challenge/` (user, 2026-10-26).
- An exemplar-first beat: show the useful output, then use AI to understand it, then the method.
- A `::: catalyst` box in the handbook where the module touches a CG workflow (more than one where needed; S01 M1 has two), with `source=` naming the Catalyst skill. Never paraphrase Catalyst as if it were Decoded's opinion. Handbook prose outside the boxes never names Catalyst (user, 2026-10-15), so the concept text stands on its own and CG specifics sit in boxes that are easy to update.
- A "Jordan" beat of one or two sentences. Not a storyline.
- **AI in the demo and in every challenge task.** The demo has one AI-assisted beat (every module after S01 M1, which teaches Copilot): plan → generate → review → validate, with its prompt in the standard 🤖 COPILOT box ("The Copilot box" below). Every challenge task is done by directing Copilot and reviewing what it wrote (below). The demo and challenge link the *Working with Copilot* appendix and never re-teach it; a handbook lists it under "Optional further reading", never as a link in its prose.
- **No inline speaker notes** (supersedes the 2026-10-14 `::: notes` ruling): everything an instructor needs lives under `instructor/` (the lesson plan first).

## Every module opens with the 🧭 anchor: What, Why and How (user, 2026-10-27)

Adult learners need to know why they're learning something before they learn it, and connect it to their own work (andragogy: need to know, experience, problem-centred, relevance). So every module opens with one consistent visual, the 🧭 module anchor (the day-level anchor is retired, user, 2026-10-27), before any picture of what will happen. It is a short recap, not a content dump.

- **Shape.** A static box, the same look everywhere: a dark header, "🧭 WHAT, WHY AND HOW", a pale band with one question from the learner's own work (e.g. "Have you ever emailed `report_final_v2.py` to a colleague, or needed last Tuesday's version back?"), then three tiles side by side, **WHAT** (the tool or idea named and defined in plain words), **WHY** (what it does for a team first, then for Jordan's report) and **HOW** (what happens in the module), each one clear sentence of at most 30 words (user, 2026-10-27: 12-word phrases stopped making sense), the same words on the slides' three tiles; the phrases stay specific: WHAT names the tool, WHY the team benefit. No fold-outs.
- **One source per session.** The words live once, in `dayNN_anchors.md` in the session folder (`## m4` …, `## together`, each with Title, Question, What, Why, How, and for modules **In short:** (the module in one line for the slides' Today tiles, user 2026-10-27); Title is for authors only and is never drawn, and is the module's canonical title; no `## day`, and nbkit ignores a stray one). It never ships. `.venv/bin/python scripts/nbkit.py anchors --session <folder>` writes every copy; `--check` and the audit fail when a copy differs, a section has none or two, or a built module has no anchor. Never edit a copy.
- **Where.** The demo's and the challenge's (and the completed challenge's) module sections and Put it together, identical in demo and challenge (in the challenge it's a recap on top of the existing sections); each handbook straight after its persona card (the persona card stays as it is, broad and separate); the overview carries none (user, 2026-10-27; nbkit removes any old one); and the lesson plan at the start of each module's section, drawn in the Word build.
- **Notebook layout of a module section.** Heading cell (the `## Module N` heading on its own) · anchor cell (generated, id `anchor-<key>`) · bridge cell ("This builds on …" naming the concrete project, numbers and repository, plus one sentence leading into the map) · module map cell with its bullets · the section's other cells. The challenge follows the same order.
- **Be specific, define first.** The What names the tool and defines it; the Why gives concrete team benefits (for Git: one shared codebase instead of copies, any earlier version back, every change reviewed before it reaches everyone); the specifics (files, commands, counts) go in the bridge.
- **Header.** The box's dark header names its job, always "🧭 WHAT, WHY AND HOW", never the module title, which the heading straight above it gives (user, 2026-10-27).
- **Twins.** `nbkit.anchor_box_html` (notebooks), the `::: {.anchor}` div drawn by `scripts/pdf-filters/module-anchor.lua` (PDF) and `anchorBox` in `scripts/docx/build_lesson_plan.js` (Word), in the same colours: frame 434343, question F3F3F3, WHAT D0E0E3, WHY FFF2CC, HOW D9EAD3.

## Learners direct AI and review what it wrote (user, 2026-10-15)

The way of working across the whole programme, and the key difference from AIA's challenges (where learners fill `___` gaps in code by hand). Every challenge, Put it together (the optional stretch in open challenge time) and the capstone follow it, and every demo models it.

- **Learners never write code or do maths by hand.** They prompt Copilot to write, fix or change the code, any calculation included, then review what it wrote, run it, and confirm from the output and a check whether it works, keeping or undoing the change. What a challenge practises is the brief, the review and the check, never typing code or working out the maths.
- **Task boxes** read "Ask Copilot to …", "Review …", "Run the check …"; never "write", "complete" or "fill in" the code.
- **Code cells and starter files** hold the starting code, complete and runnable, with its planted fault where the task has one, for Copilot to work on at the learner's direction. A `___` gap only records what the learner observed or decided (an error type, a line number, Keep or Undo and why), in markdown or a string; never code to write. No `# TODO` for the learner to code, no "Step N" headings, no "fill the gaps".
- **Light maths.** Tasks centre on reading, reviewing and fixing Python with AI help: a traceback, Copilot `/explain`, reviewing a draft script, Keep or Undo, a recorded check against a known number. A calculation the task needs is Copilot's to write and the learner's to check.
- **Folded solutions** show a model prompt with its Copilot settings (the 🤖 COPILOT box's badges and text block, below), the change a good answer makes (the fixed code) and the check that proves it, then the reflect answer: `nbkit.reflect_solution(question, nbkit.copilot_solution(prompt, change, check, mode=…, model=…, attach=…), answer)`.
- **Demos model it.** The AI-assisted beat shows the prompt in a 🤖 COPILOT box, the review of every generated line and the check, so the challenge asks for nothing the demo did not show.
- The boxes, their exact HTML and the helpers that build them: `lesson-build/references/notebook-boxes.md` and `scripts/nbkit.py`; the challenge's shape: `module-challenge`. `module-audit` warns on code gaps, TODOs, "Step N" headings and "fill the gaps" in a challenge.

## The Copilot box (user, 2026-10-16)

Wherever learners or instructors are told to prompt Copilot, the prompt sits in one standard box, in the same way that the handbook appears only in the one Go deeper box. It says at a glance where to open Copilot and how to set it up, gives the prompt in a block learners copy, and says what to do with the answer, so the box covers every way learners use Copilot: Chat view (the Chat panel), Inline chat (in the editor) and Terminal chat (in the terminal) (user, 2026-10-16). It is the same box in every file: the demo, the challenge and its folded solutions, the handbooks, the lesson plan and the overview.

- **What it is.** A dark-grey frame with a blue header, "🤖 COPILOT — <what it should do>" (the job in a few words: "Turn the cells into a script"); colour-coded badges in the order **WHERE** on aqua (the surface to open), **MODE** on yellow (Chat view only), **MODEL** on blue and, when the prompt needs context, **ATTACH** on green, each label in dark ink on its colour and each value on white, so learners tell them apart at a glance; an optional one-line purpose; then the prompt in a plain ```` ```text ```` block; then the closing line, which follows the surface. Chat view's follows the mode. Ask: check the answer against the code, which is the final word. Plan: read the plan before anything changes. Agent: read every line it wrote, choose **Keep** or **Undo**, and run the check. Inline chat: read the change in the editor, choose **Keep** or **Undo**, and run the check. Inline chat in a notebook cell (2026-10-16) is the exception: VS Code shows the change as a diff in the cell with **Accept and Run**, which applies it and reruns the cell (code.visualstudio.com/docs/agents/guides/notebooks-with-ai), so the box keeps WHERE Inline chat and sets its own line with `then=`: read the diff in the cell; if it makes only the change you asked for, choose **Accept and Run**; if not, discard it and ask again (`nbkit.COPILOT_THEN_NOTEBOOK_CELL`, or a line fitted to the task). It names no reject button, because the docs name none. A box for a `.py` file in the editor keeps the Keep or Undo default. Terminal chat: choose **Insert**, not Run: read the command, press Enter, and check what it printed.
- **When to use it.** Every prompt a learner or instructor is given to type or paste: the demo's AI-assisted beat, a challenge task that hands learners a prompt, the model prompt in every folded solution, a handbook's worked prompt (a `/explain` question, a five-part brief) and a prompt the lesson plan quotes. Where the task is for learners to write their own prompt (a brief), the task box asks for it and the folded solution shows the model one. A prompt is never a blockquote, never introduced by a prose label such as "(Copilot Chat, Ask mode)" and never a bare fenced block without the box.
- **One box per cell** (user, 2026-10-16): in a notebook each 🤖 COPILOT box, with its prompt block and closing line, is its own markdown cell with nothing else in it; a task that needs two prompts gets two cells, and any lead-in sits in the cell before.
- **The settings vocabulary**, chosen from the Module 1 handbook's guidance on modes and models. Use these values exactly; `module-audit` warns on anything else.

  | Badge | Values | Choose |
  |---|---|---|
  | WHERE | Chat view, Inline chat, Terminal chat | **Chat view**, the Chat panel, opened with Ctrl+Alt+I (Ctrl+Cmd+I on a Mac), for questions, plans and jobs that change files: the default, and the only surface with a mode; **Inline chat**, opened with Ctrl+I (Cmd+I on a Mac) on lines selected in the editor, for one change to lines you can see, shown in place as a diff, then Keep or Undo (in a notebook cell, **Accept and Run**, which also reruns the cell); **Terminal chat**, opened with Ctrl+I (Cmd+I on a Mac) after a click in the terminal, for a shell command to read before it runs, so Insert, not Run. Ctrl+I opens Inline chat or Terminal chat, whichever has focus, so select or click first. In a file with Chat view edits not yet kept or undone (VS Code: a file in an active chat editing session), Ctrl+I opens the prompt in the Chat view instead, as Ask in Chat; Keep or Undo those edits first (the setting `inlineChat.askInChat`, set to false, turns this off; code.visualstudio.com/docs/chat/inline-chat, checked 2026-10-16). Mac shortcuts are written in words, never as key symbols, which the PDF fonts can't print. The legend's line under its intro gives the same rule for choosing: shell command, Terminal chat; one change to lines you can see, Inline chat; anything else, Chat view |
  | MODE | Ask, Plan, Agent (Chat view only, where it is required) | **Ask** to understand code (`/explain`, questions; nothing changes); **Plan** for a bigger change, to see the steps before anything changes; **Agent** for a job that creates or changes files |
  | MODEL | Fast, Reasoning, Auto | **Fast** for quick edits and questions; **Reasoning** for multi-step work; **Auto** where Copilot may pick, where the organisation allows it |
  | ATTACH | `#<file>` (`#report.ipynb`), `selection`, `#codebase`, `#terminalLastCommand`, `#terminalSelection`; several in one badge, separated by spaces | Only when the prompt needs context the open file doesn't give: a named file, the selected lines, a search of the whole workspace, or what the terminal printed (the last command and its output, or the lines selected in the terminal) for a Chat view question about an error. Inline chat takes no `selection`: it works on the selected lines already, so the selection is where it opens, not context to add, and the box's purpose line says which lines to select |

- **Short prompt lines.** Keep each line of a prompt under about 90 characters so it doesn't wrap in the PDF: one line per part of a brief, split into more lines where a part runs long.
- **Notebooks** (demo and challenge anchors): `md(nbkit.copilot_prompt(title, prompt, mode, model, attach=None, purpose=None, then=None, where="Chat view"))` for each prompt (`mode=None` with `where="Inline chat"` or `"Terminal chat"`, and `""` counts as no mode; the helpers raise `ValueError` on an unknown surface, a mode given with Inline chat or Terminal chat, or a missing mode with Chat view), and `md(nbkit.copilot_legend())` once, in "How to use this notebook" or the cell straight after it, in every anchor that has a box, so each badge is explained once. Folded solutions give their model prompt the same badges and text block: `nbkit.reflect_solution(question, nbkit.copilot_solution(prompt, change, check, mode=…, model=…, attach=…, where=…), answer)`, where `mode` defaults to `None`: Ask in Chat view, and no mode in Inline chat or Terminal chat. A Markdown anchor pastes the `*_html` twins (`copilot_prompt_html`, `copilot_legend_html`).
- **Markdown that becomes a PDF** (handbooks, the lesson plan, the overview): a `::: copilot` div around a ```` ```text ```` block, which `scripts/pdf-filters/copilot-box.lua` draws as the same box with the same closing line (without the 🤖, which the PDF fonts can't print). `title`, `where` and `model` are required, `mode` with `where="Chat view"` only, and `attach` is optional; a div with no `where` draws as Chat view, and the audit warns. `mode=""` counts as no mode; an empty or unknown mode, or one given with Inline chat or Terminal chat, warns on stderr and is left out, never drawn as a badge. A paragraph before the code block becomes the one-line purpose; `then="…"` replaces the closing line and `then="none"` leaves it out.

  ````markdown
  ::: {.copilot title="Explain the report function" where="Chat view" mode="Ask" model="Fast" attach="selection"}
  ```text
  /explain What does build_report return, and in what order?
  ```
  :::

  ::: {.copilot title="Find the biggest files" where="Terminal chat" model="Fast"}
  ```text
  List the five largest files under this folder, biggest first.
  ```
  :::
  ````

  The Word lesson plan (`scripts/docx/build_lesson_plan.js`) draws the same badges and closing lines from the same div.

- The exact HTML and the templates: `lesson-build/references/notebook-boxes.md` → "Copilot box", "Copilot box in Inline chat", "Copilot box in Terminal chat" and "Copilot settings legend"; the helpers are in `scripts/nbkit.py`. `module-audit` warns on a prompt outside the box (a bare text block or a blockquote), an anchor with boxes but no legend, a folded solution whose prompt is a blockquote, an incomplete `::: copilot` div (one with no `where` included), a div whose `then=` doesn't fit its surface, badge values outside the vocabulary, a badge not in nbkit's exact spelling (colour included), Accept or Discard as Inline chat's buttons (they are Keep and Undo; **Accept and Run**, a notebook cell's button, passes) and a Mac key symbol in content, where the shortcut belongs in words.

## Writing rules (user, 2026-10-15)

- **Handbook prose is connected text.** Write connected paragraphs that each develop one idea over several sentences (usually three to six), with a transition that carries the reader from one paragraph to the next. Avoid runs of one- and two-sentence paragraphs, which read as disjointed notes. A flow or a comparison still becomes a figure or a table, and a Jordan beat is woven into a paragraph rather than standing alone. This overrides Decoded's short-paragraph house rule for Core handbooks, and the same applies to the prose in `dayNN_overview.md`.
- **No in-line references** in handbook or overview prose: no links, citations or asides such as "(VS Code docs, …)" inside a paragraph, a list or a caption. A handbook's sources appear only in each section's "Optional further reading" list, and the prose paraphrases the documentation rather than quoting it. Two kinds of content are not in-line references. The `::: catalyst` box quotes Catalyst with `source=` because CG guidance must not be paraphrased. The overview's "Before you start" may point to the handbooks and the `demo/` and `challenge/` folders, because that is navigation, not a source.
- **Demos keep bullets and the lesson plan keeps short spoken lines.** The connected-paragraph rule is for reading text; a demo beat is three to five bullets under its visual, and a lesson-plan row is lean: up to three short notes and up to two lines the instructor says in one breath (user, 2026-10-16).
- **"The proper words" only where the visual doesn't already show them.** If a figure or diagram already labels the analogy against the technical terms (the lifecycle card, the bench and recipe card, the kernel diagram, the four-moves diagram), the text does not repeat that mapping as a "The proper words" line. Use the line only for a complex idea whose analogy the visual does not label. This applies to handbooks and demos.
- **Demos are visual-first.** The handbook carries the depth; the demo lets the instructor explain the core of each idea with visuals. A module's demo section opens with its heading, its 🧭 anchor, a bridge and its module map, each in its own cell, then gives each beat one visual (a brand concept card in the Decoded palette made by a script in `scripts/figures/dayNN/`, or a Mermaid diagram for a flow), three to five bullets, and a short code cell only when the beat runs something. Where two things are being contrasted, the visual shows them side by side, and a figure that pairs two things (a file's lines and what each makes) lines them up row by row, in the same order (user, 2026-10-27). A config file the demo opens (a Dockerfile, a pipeline YAML) gets a plain line-by-line table in the file's own order: the line, and what it does (user, 2026-10-27). Modelled on AIA Unit 4 Session 16's demo.
- **Each challenge section shows one recap visual** of the demo section it follows (usually the demo's module map or its key card, reused), after its 🧭 anchor and bridge and before the tasks.
- **Be specific** (user, 2026-10-27; Decoded-wide in `courseware-conventions`). A reference back names the file, the command and what it shows, never "a check" or "a saved copy".
- **Define first** (user, 2026-10-27; Decoded-wide). Every term and method is named and defined at first use in each file, before any shorthand (test-driven development (TDD) before "test-first"; fixture, pin, squash merge, pull request), because each file can be read on its own.
- **Name the testing levels where they occur** (user, 2026-10-27): unit, integration and end-to-end smoke tests, and what each is good for.
- **Keep it simple** (user, 2026-10-26): the profile's CG rule 10 is the one statement (S02: one `requirements.txt`, not two; one branch rule, extended in Module 6; the challenge pipeline runs the tests only).

## Handbook numbering, handbook mentions, naming and formatting (user, 2026-10-15)

- **The PDF numbers handbook sections and figures; the Markdown never does.** Headings are plain (`## Notebooks, scripts and how Python runs your code`); `scripts/pdf-filters/handbook-section-numbers.lua` numbers H2s 1, 2, 3 … and H3s 1.1, 1.2 … in each handbook whose H1 id ends in `-handbook`, and skips `{.unnumbered}` headings such as the Summary. An image's caption is its description (`![Caption text.](../../../../common/figures/dayNN/mN-<what>.png){width=90%}`, no italic "*Figure 1.2: …*" line); a Mermaid block's caption is `<!-- caption: "…" -->` straight after its closing fence. Prose never cites a figure or section by number: "the chart below", "the four moves", never "Figure 1.2" or "section 1.4". Supersedes the numbered `## 1.1` sections and the "*Figure 1.N*" caption line.
- **The handbook is mentioned in a demo or challenge only in one Go deeper box**, at the end of each module section of each anchor, in its own markdown cell (a Markdown anchor pastes the same HTML as its own block). Build it with `nbkit.go_deeper(N, body)`; the spelling is fixed, from AIA's demos, and only the body sentence changes:

  ```html
  <div style="border:2px solid #F8DB59; border-radius:8px; margin:10px 0;">
  <div style="background:#F8DB59; color:#000; padding:8px 14px; font-weight:bold;">📚 GO DEEPER &nbsp;—&nbsp; the Module N handbook</div>
  <div style="background:#fffef5; padding:12px 14px; color:#1a1a1a;">One or two sentences: what the handbook adds (the full explanation, more worked examples, further reading).</div></div>
  ```

  The box names "the Module N handbook" only: no path, no link, no section title or number, because such references go stale whenever the handbook changes and learners find topics through the PDF's contents page. Nothing else in the demo, the challenge, the files beside them or any image they show mentions the handbook (beat titles, module maps and alt text included). The lesson plan and the overview may point to "the Module N handbook" as a whole but never cite a section by number or title. The put-it-together section has no box.
- **Naming.** "Challenge" names the learner activity, its folder, its anchor and its data; "practice" is retired from every name, path and timing (plain English such as "in practice" stays). The data is `courseware/common/data/demo/` ("the demo data", HLF) and `courseware/common/data/challenge/` ("the challenge data", HLP). Session files carry the `dayNN_` prefix; files beside the anchors keep module prefixes (`m1_…`, `together_…`); a module may add a named folder. Nothing is zipped for now; say "the course folder", never "the download" or "the zip".
- **Titles** (user, 2026-10-27). Each unit, day and module has one canonical title, the programme overview's (`internal-programme-overview.md`, "Programme at a glance"), word for word in every heading, breadcrumb, lesson-plan section, overview, handbook H1, the anchors' Title, slide and agenda; never shortened or re-cased ("Software engineering with AI", never "with ai"). A long title gets a smaller font on a slide, never a cut.
