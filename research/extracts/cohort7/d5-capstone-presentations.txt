        Group 1 Portfolio Summary and Advisor Book
        • The team reported building a phase-one model with staging and integration
          models, final outputs, reconciliation passes, data-quality checks, build tests,
          and the required Airflow DAGs.
        • Their source data included trading data, CRM, fund references, security, and
          portfolio management.
        • Their consumer-facing product was described as supporting advisory
          leadership and portfolio oversight.
        • The team reported seven source-aligned tables and approximately nine ETL
          models. The presentation also referred to seven sources; these counts may
          reflect different layers of the design.
        • The two selected outputs were Portfolio Summary and Advisor Book, intended
          to help users assess portfolio health, including assets under management.
        • The team reported running two jobs and said the Airflow jobs, Databricks
          catalogs, and resulting data could be inspected.
        “We basically tackled … Portfolio summary and advisor book.
        Data-quality observation
        • The team found trades dated before a portfolio s inception date.
        • They chose to retain those records and notify business users, since they did
          not know whether the dates indicated bad data or a valid business situation.
        • A data-quality check could be added if business users identify the records as
          an issue; otherwise, a business explanation may justify keeping them.
        “We decided, hey, let's let our business users know that this is happening, and
        we will just keep it in there.

    • The team described a pipeline that takes raw seed data through staging and
      domain-specific processing to consumer-aligned outputs.
    • Transformations considered included retaining valid trades, handling price
      conversion, and aggregating the data.
    • Potential issues they identified included:
      ◦ Whether the latest price is fair and current.
      ◦ Invalid trades, fee calculations, and reconciliation.
      ◦ Missing currency conversion, which could make traded-value comparisons
         inaccurate.
      ◦ Trades referencing unknown instruments, which could be excluded by a join.
      ◦ Duplicate counting if the same trade appears more than once.
      ◦ Inconsistent or incorrect segment labels.
    • For positions, the team described calculating net buys and sales for each
      portfolio and instrument. The transcript s wording is unclear about the exact
      formula.
    Group 3 Portfolio Summary and Advisor Book
    • This team also selected Portfolio Summary and Advisor Book, describing the
      goal as a trustworthy view of portfolios and advisor books.
    • They worked backward from the outputs to identify source data. Sources
      mentioned included advisor and CRM data, trades, and fund summary or report
      data.
    • The team described using seven seed datasets to produce consumer reports
      and presented an ERD to support team discussion and PRD development.
    • They said they clarified definitions that were unclear in the requirements and
      identified data-quality validation needs.
    • Their presentation also called out data ownership, approvals, and validation.
    • Databricks was proposed as the platform for the dbt work.
