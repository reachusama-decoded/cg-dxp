# Audit checklist — the items a script cannot judge

Work through these after `scripts/audit.py` is clean (0 errors, 0 warnings), in development-flow order. The session-wide items (process, timing, environment, read-through) are in `module-builder/references/session-checklist.md`.

## Scope and structure
- [ ] Every key question in the brief's module section is in the overview's `::: session` list and answered in the handbook (concepts) or the challenge (hands-on).
- [ ] The handbook runs exemplar first: the useful output, then AI to understand it, then the methods.
- [ ] The overview's outcomes match `internal-programme-overview.md` §6 verbatim, with no module tags on outcomes, questions or self-check items; the self-check has at most three yes-or-no questions, one per module in module order, each about the prior knowledge that most changes that module's pace, and the lesson plan's welcome says which question paces which module and what to adjust; the lesson-plan block names the outcomes the module serves and maps the questions; each outcome clause's core is evidenced at Q1 or Q2, and a clause evidenced only in the demo or debrief says so in the Objectives table; its debrief walks through the solution in `challenge-solution/` or asks two or three questions that surface blockers.
- [ ] The outcomes are `internal-programme-overview.md` section 6's, word for word, in every place (user, 2026-10-16): the overview, the lesson plan's Objectives table and every partial quote in the handbooks, the anchors, the completed challenge and the files beside them. `audit.py` catches the copies and the quotes of six or more words; read for what it cannot, a Say line or reflect answer that restates an outcome in other words and no longer promises the same thing. A change is made in all of them in the same edit.
- [ ] The module fits its hour (user, 2026-10-16): demo about 20, challenge 20 to 25 in two rows (a brief, then learners working), debrief 5, break 5 to 10; a module may run a little faster or slower by complexity, keeping the pattern. Its lesson-plan rows (2.5 to 20 minutes, in 2.5-minute steps, start times h:mm or h:mm:ss on a half minute; user, 2026-10-27) sum to it. Each demo row fits its minutes on a real run; slow steps (a push, a pipeline run, a first image build) are pre-run in the break, and the break row and the demo row's note say so; paired challenges run in parallel and swap only for the review.
- [ ] One lesson-plan row per demo heading (user, 2026-10-27): every `### ` heading of the demo's module section is exactly one lesson-plan row, with the same title and in the same order, nothing skipped or grouped; the closing ("Where the report goes next") is the last lines of the final beat, before CHALLENGE TIME, not a heading; the module's section title in the plan equals the demo's `## Module N — …` heading; the challenge rows name the challenge's `## … challenge — …` title and its Q1, Q2 and Q3 task titles exactly; rows of 2.5 to 20 minutes in 2.5-minute steps, start times h:mm (or h:mm:ss on a half minute); the audit fails on drift (user, 2026-10-27). `audit.py` checks the titles, the order and the counts; read whether each row's note and Say (each opening with its heading in bold) fit their beat, and whether the numbers match the executed demo.
- [ ] The rows teach from the back of the room: a line of purpose, on screen, transition and assessment, up to three short notes, up to two brief Say lines in Decoded's voice, Adapt only where it really matters; no Key questions in rows; no timed check-ins or warnings while learners work; the debrief walks through the solution or asks questions that surface blockers.
- [ ] Put it together is the optional stretch for fast finishers, inside the last block of open challenge time, never a block everyone does; the start and end of the day take 30 minutes on the template: the 15-minute intro (Meet and greet, Jordan and the day, What you will be able to do; no Setup check row), the closing debrief, Key takeaways and Thank you for participating (user, 2026-10-27).
- [ ] No placeholder sections for modules not yet built, in any file.
- [ ] The overview runs in order (user, 2026-10-19): a one- or two-sentence opening that introduces Jordan, the persona card (no 🧭 anchor; user, 2026-10-27), the session tied to Jordan's report, how the day runs (open challenge time optional), the questions before the outcomes, Before you start (What's in this session, with the "Module N handbook: concepts in depth" rows and no demo row, since demos are instructor-only; Set up at the start of the day, with the Services line; What you already know), the agenda (outlined, no times, the Welcome row "Meet and greet, Jordan and the day, what you will be able to do", each module by its canonical title, an "Open challenge time (optional)" row and a Closing debrief row), How the challenges work (the outlined tier table with its badges, how to read the tiers, never what each task is), then The data.
- [ ] Each handbook opens with its intro paragraph, the persona card and `## What this handbook covers {#dayNN-mN-covers .unnumbered}`; every further-reading item reads `- **Type:** [*Title*](url) by Source — what it adds.`; nothing in a handbook row, a Go deeper box or a table says when to read the handbook. The module's 🧭 anchor follows the persona card. Any appendix follows the Summary, opens by saying nothing in it is needed for the module, its exercises or its outcomes, and nothing in the main text depends on it.
- [ ] The lesson plan's details a script can't judge: it opens "Timings are a guide: adapt them to the room."; the checklist is in three groups, Before the day, On the day and After rehearsing, opening with "copy the course folder to your laptop" and naming no platform; Materials opens with the line on paths from the session's `instructor/` and `learner/` folders and gives the layout's paths (`dayNN_mN_challenge_solution.pdf` in `instructor/`, `.devcontainer/`, `common/services/<name>/`, `common/data/demo/`, `common/data/challenge/`); the learner setup check says learners set up at the start of the day, as they arrive, checked at the end of the welcome; no slide references.
- [ ] `courseware/unit00-programme/learner/start-here.md` covers every unit (u1 to u5), says Dev Containers is required and why, has a general "Start a session's services" section and setup at the start of each session, and never names Google Drive or any platform, or mentions downloading or unzipping.

## Flow and connection
- [ ] Names, numbers and order in the lesson plan, overview, demo and challenge trace to the handbook; nothing downstream contradicts it.
- [ ] Every number and output the handbook, lesson plan or anchors quote matches a real run in the dev container image (demo anchor outputs, folded solutions run from fresh starter files), re-run after any pin change and never hand-edited.
- [ ] Each lesson-plan row's Transition leads into the next row, and the module's Break row's Transition is the bridge into the next module, matching that module's section summary.
- [ ] Each module section's 🧭 anchor and bridge make the case for the module from the learner's own work and name what it builds on (project, numbers, repository), and the artefact grows module by module (S01: Jordan's weekly fund report). The anchors in `dayNN_anchors.md` read well and define their terms; the copies are generated.
- [ ] The anchors say when to open each file beside them, what to look at, and when to come back; the lesson plan's rows name the same sections, visuals and COPILOT boxes (never cell numbers).
- [ ] Each anchor's 🗺️ table of contents reads well at the top of the notebook (user, 2026-10-16): its What happens items are what a learner scans for. Where a row reads oddly, reword the `### ` heading or box title it comes from and rerun `scripts/nbkit.py toc --nb`; never edit the table by hand.
- [ ] Every dataset is introduced by a data card, never a paragraph, given once at the top of each anchor (its `## Setup` section, before the first module) with one file and what it holds per line in the Files row: the overview's cards and the anchors' `nbkit.data_card` cells (the blue 🗂️ DATA box, the persona card's design; user, 2026-10-19) give the same fields for the same dataset (the Files row included), and the lesson plan says what to say over each card the anchor shows (user, 2026-10-15).
- [ ] The overview's "Services this session uses" line matches the services the anchors and the lesson plan actually start, and each service's README lists the session.
- [ ] The handbook stands alone (user, 2026-10-16): it never names a module, another module's headings, files, figures or coined terms as if the reader knew them, and it explains briefly what it uses from elsewhere or describes it in general words; Jordan's report is the thread, in this handbook's own terms. The script catches "Module N", "the previous module", "earlier in the session", another module's `mN_` files and `figures/mN-` figures and the terms in `COINED_TERMS`; read for the rest ("you saw" pointing at another file rather than at a plot on the page, "as before", another handbook's heading or idea named in other words). When a handbook coins a term a later one might lean on, add it to `COINED_TERMS` in `scripts/audit.py`.

## The handbook and its numbers (user, 2026-10-15)
- [ ] No image in the demo or challenge (module map, concept card, recap visual) shows the handbook's name or a section number; the script reads only alt text.
- [ ] Each Go deeper box's body says what the handbook adds (the full explanation, more worked examples, further reading), in one or two sentences, cites no section by title and never says when to read it.
- [ ] The rendered PDF's contents page numbers the handbook's sections 1, 2, 3 … and 1.1, 1.2 …, the Summary unnumbered, and every figure has a number and its caption (after the user's review, when the PDF is built).
- [ ] Catalyst is named only inside `::: catalyst` boxes, and the prose around them still makes sense without them.

## CG and honesty
- [ ] One AI-assisted beat in the demo (after S01 M1), its prompt in a 🤖 COPILOT box, appendix linked not re-taught; it shows the prompt, the review and the check the challenge will ask for.
- [ ] Harbourline, Jordan (they/them), the synthetic datasets; no real firm, no real person, no CG-internal detail that is not from Catalyst.
- [ ] Every `::: catalyst` box quotes and names its source; nothing "at CG" is guessed.
- [ ] Where tools, platforms or ways of working are discussed, they are compared with their pros and cons and what each is good for; nothing is framed as a tool's limitation or weakness, in table headings ("Good for", "Trade-offs") and FAQs too; review and checks read as good practice every change gets, not as a fix for a tool's flaws; no `<!-- sensitive: … -->` marker anywhere (`programme-profile` CG rule 2); tone constructive.
- [ ] No CG-access notes anywhere (no "Needs CG access" section, `::: blocked` callout, "until CG answers" caveat, mirror-container fallback, "check on a CG laptop", Asana task or "the CG dev container"); every demo and challenge runs on the dev container and the local services today (`programme-profile` rule 3).
- [ ] Delivery mechanics are in-person and virtual-safe: no chat votes, Menti, breakouts; every activity works alone on a laptop.
- [ ] Every "think of it like…" analogy has a figure. A "The proper words" line appears only where the figure does not already label the analogy against the technical terms (user, 2026-10-15); where the figure labels it, the line is gone.
- [ ] Every external link resolves and appears in the module's `references.md` with the date it was checked; every date, incident and statistic is sourced or removed.
- [ ] No learner-facing text names a later session or module as where a skill is taught (the overview may list the pair); the lesson plan may.

## Writing and visuals (user, 2026-10-15)
- [ ] Handbook and overview prose reads as connected text when read aloud: each paragraph develops one idea over several sentences, transitions carry the reader on, no runs of short paragraphs the script missed.
- [ ] No in-line references anywhere in handbook or overview prose; every source sits in the section's "Optional further reading"; documentation is paraphrased, not quoted (the `::: catalyst` box excepted).
- [ ] Every reference back names the file, the command and what it shows; every term is defined at first use before any shorthand, in each file on its own; testing levels are named where tests appear; every design is the simplest version that works.
- [ ] The demo section's heading, anchor, bridge and module map each sit in their own cell, in that order; every beat has one visual and three to five bullets; every config file the demo opens has a line-by-line table in the file's order; a figure that pairs two things lines them up row by row; contrasts are side by side; code appears only where a beat runs something; every card has its script in `scripts/figures/dayNN/`.
- [ ] Each challenge section shows one recap visual after its anchor and bridge and before its first task, matching what the demo showed.

## Copilot prompts (user, 2026-10-16)
- [ ] Every prompt learners or instructors are given, in any file, sits in the standard 🤖 COPILOT box (`programme-profile/references/conventions.md` → "The Copilot box"); the script catches prompts in blockquotes and bare text blocks only where the words around them say "prompt", "ask Copilot", "/explain" or "brief", so read for the rest (a prompt in a table cell, in a bullet, or introduced as "type this").
- [ ] Each box's WHERE fits its job (user, 2026-10-16): Chat view for questions, plans and jobs that change files; Inline chat for one change to the selected code that the learner reads in the diff, then keeps or undoes (**Keep** or **Undo**), or in a notebook cell applies and reruns (**Accept and Run**); Terminal chat for one shell command the learner reads, Inserts (not Runs) and checks. A box that is really an inline or terminal job is not left on Chat view, and a box whose closing line was set by hand (`then=`) still says what to do on its surface: the script catches a mode, Keep or Undo on Terminal chat and Run or Insert on Inline chat, so read for the rest (a line that would make sense only in the Chat view).
- [ ] Each Inline chat box closes for where the change lands (2026-10-16): a box on a notebook cell (the anchor's own cells, a challenge task's "select your line in the cell") closes with **Accept and Run** (`then=nbkit.COPILOT_THEN_NOTEBOOK_CELL` or a line fitted to the task) and names no reject button, and its task text says Accept and Run too; a box on a `.py` file in the editor keeps Keep or Undo. The script can't tell which a box targets, so read the purpose line and the task. Where Ctrl+I follows an Agent edit to the same file, the task says to Keep or Undo those edits first: Ctrl+I in a file with Chat view edits not yet kept or undone opens the Chat view instead.
- [ ] Each box's settings fit its job, from the handbook's guidance: Ask for understanding code (`/explain`, questions), Plan for a bigger change before anything changes, Agent for a job that creates or changes files; Fast for quick edits and questions, Reasoning for multi-step work, Auto only where Copilot may pick; ATTACH wherever the prompt relies on a file, the selected lines, the whole codebase or what the terminal printed (`#terminalLastCommand`, `#terminalSelection`, for a Chat view question about a command's output), and nowhere else.
- [ ] Running text names the surfaces as the badge does, capitalised: Chat view, Inline chat, Terminal chat (a citation of VS Code's docs may keep the docs' own "editor inline chat" and "terminal inline chat"); shortcuts are spelt out with the Mac in brackets, "Ctrl+I (Cmd+I on a Mac)", "Ctrl+Alt+I (Ctrl+Cmd+I on a Mac)", Insert as "Alt+Enter (Option+Enter on a Mac)" and Run as "Ctrl+Enter (Cmd+Enter on a Mac)"; Inline chat's buttons are Keep and Undo in the editor and **Accept and Run** in a notebook cell (the script flags ⌘, ⌃, ⌥, ⇧ and a bare Accept or Discard).
- [ ] The box's title names the job in a few words; its purpose line (if any) adds something the title doesn't; the text after a box doesn't repeat its closing line.
- [ ] No prompt line runs past about 90 characters (it would wrap in the PDF); a brief has one line per part.
- [ ] A prompt quoted in the lesson plan matches the anchor's box word for word, settings included.

## Challenge (user, 2026-10-15)
- [ ] Demo ≠ challenge: the demo reads the demo data, the challenge the challenge data, and the task asks its own question and plants its own faults, none of them the demo's.
- [ ] The way of working holds (`programme-profile/references/conventions.md` → "Learners direct AI and review what it wrote"): every task line asks Copilot to write, fix or change the code, or asks the learner to review it or run the check; nothing has the learner type code or work out maths; `___` only records what they saw or decided; light maths.
- [ ] The tiers ramp and nothing is mandatory: learners go as far as they can, starting at Q1; Q1 is reachable by most in the challenge's minutes (the demo's 🏆 CHALLENGE TIME box), Q2 asks for real review, Q3 is not expected; the lesson plan names the three likeliest stalls and the task boxes or hints cover them.
- [ ] Each planted fault is findable by reading and running the code, fails or misleads exactly where the task says and nowhere else, and is not given away by a comment, a name or a box; the lesson plan lists the faults for instructors. A planted-fault file is a first draft to review, never Copilot's mistakes (CG rule 2).
- [ ] Every folded solution's prompt, typed into Copilot, plausibly gives its change; applying the change to fresh starter files and running its check gives the quoted number; each reflect question asks why, and its answer is in the solution.
- [ ] Self-contained: a learner who ran nothing in the demo can do the whole challenge from its own setup cell and starter files (no demo variable relied on, which the script cannot see).
- [ ] The demo's 🏆 CHALLENGE TIME box names the same tiers as the challenge's task boxes, and the minutes of the module's challenge rows in the lesson plan (S01: 25).
- [ ] Prerequisite-honest for the pair; a solo route for paired work.
- [ ] The put-it-together task uses every built module's skill, the same boxes and way of working, and ships the session's artefact; it is the optional stretch for fast finishers in open challenge time, and says so. It starts from a state a learner who stopped at Q1 or Q2 of each module reaches, named in its first lines.
