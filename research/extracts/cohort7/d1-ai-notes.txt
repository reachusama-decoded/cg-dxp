Data Products
Definition and perspectives
• A data product can be understood as a dataset accompanied by promises
  about its use and quality. Common properties include:
  ◦ Trusted
  ◦ Reusable
  ◦ Business-ready
  ◦ Accessible
• Promises can be documented in design materials and contracts, then enforced
  through automated quality checks and code running on supporting
  infrastructure.
• From an engineering perspective, a product includes the data, transformations,
  code, and infrastructure that implement its promises.
• From a consumer or product perspective, the focus is on the usefulness of the
  data and the needs it serves.
• Merely placing a third-party dataset, such as Morningstar data, in a folder does
  not by itself make it a Capital Group data product.


Consumer alignment and value
• A data product transforms source-aligned data into consumer-aligned data.
• Source-aligned data reflects the provider s interests and structure. Consumer-




  aligned data is shaped around the needs and objectives of its intended users.
                         ’
• Source-aligned data reflects the provider s interests and structure. Consumer-




  aligned data is shaped around the needs and objectives of its intended users.
• Alignment is relative to people s goals and needs; it is not a technical property
  that can be established solely through code or automated tests.
• Much of a data product s value comes from transforming data to meet
  consumer needs while making it dependable and accessible.
• Engineering supports both realignment through transformations and business
  rules, and the enforcement of promises such as quality standards and SLAs.


What a data product is not
• It is not merely a source dataset.
• It is not a consumer-aligned dataset left without supporting processes.
• It is not code and infrastructure without meaningful transformation, consumer
  alignment, and enforced promises.


Early-Stage Data-Product Design
Initial discovery and triage
• Clarify who needs the product, its business purpose, whether it already exists,
  what people do now (including workarounds), and whether it is still needed.
• Assess whether and how it can be built, including whether the data exists, its
  sources, required transformations, platform, data grain, sensitivity controls,
  delivery method, update frequency, adoption, and timing.
• Consider why consumers would use the product instead of existing options.
Before deciding to build, consider viability, value, cost, and appropriate
prioritization. The facilitator described this early decision-making as the triage
stage.

Requirements documents
• A product requirements document PRD) describes the product s purpose and
  requirements.
• A data contract gives more specific expectations for data, such as identity,
  schema, SLAs, data quality, dependencies, glossary, and change management.
• A glossary is useful in a data contract because teams and sources may use the
  same terms differently; upstream providers may define their own terminology.
• Requirements should reflect consumer and producer input. AI-generated
  documents can help draft artifacts but do not replace stakeholder
  understanding.

Catalyst and AI Assisted Work
Catalyst skills
• Catalyst was described as a collection of AI skills for work across the data
  product lifecycle, from design documentation to technical artifacts such as
  Python and DBT.
• Skills are reusable instructions, often stored as Markdown prompts, that guide
  the AI to produce artifacts in consistent structures aligned with company policy
  and standards.
• A data-contract skill can generate a structured document covering identity,
  schema, SLAs, and data quality.
• Lifecycle skills can call more specific skills in sequence, including requirements
  and acceptance criteria, feasibility analysis, consumer use cases, and data
  modeling.
• Some skills include Python scripts that allow operations such as searching
  Databricks or running jobs.

Inference, reasoning, tools, and agents
• Training creates a model; inference is the process of running it.
• The instructor explained that some modern systems generate hidden
  intermediate tokens, often called reasoning, before producing a visible
  response. This can increase inference cost.
• Output quality may depend on whether training data contains useful examples
  of the relevant reasoning or steps. Shared explanatory material, such as code
  examples or technical discussions, can provide such examples.
• A tool call is structured text, often JSON, that a program can interpret as an
  instruction to perform an action.
• A harness interprets model tool calls and performs actions. VS Code and
  Claude Code were given as examples of interfaces or harnesses; Opus was
  given as an example of a model.
• An agent was described as a harness combined with an LLM: the model
  produces actions and the harness executes them.
• In this framing, AI skills are prompts that can guide an LLM to generate text or
  trigger tool calls.

  described them as differing in size, compression, capability, and cost.

• The “thinking effort control was described as changing the amount of hidden

Interview structure
• The exercise used three roles: data producer, data consumer, and data
  creator.
• The producer should know the data source well; the consumer should know
  what they need; the creator asks questions to determine how to shape the
  product.
• The interview was intended to establish what should be built after the group
  had considered whether building the product was worthwhile and feasible.
• Suggested questions included:
  ◦ What decision do you make with the data?
  ◦ How do you make that decision today?
  ◦ Who uses the result directly?
  ◦ How is the source generated?
  ◦ What do these terms mean in your work?
  ◦ How do you recognize the result is usable?
  ◦ When do you read the result?

  understanding the consumer s needs.
• Groups were asked to record the discussion and use the transcript with
  Catalyst to draft a PRD and data contract. Participants were encouraged to stay
  in role but could help one another if the roleplay stalled.
